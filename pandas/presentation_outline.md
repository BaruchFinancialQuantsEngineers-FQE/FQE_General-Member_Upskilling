# Three-Minute Presentation Outline

Use this outline for your case study slide at **Data Case Study on Thursday, October 22**. You have three minutes and one slide. Every section below maps to one part of that slide or your spoken delivery.

## Format

| Part | Time | What to say or show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Question | 20 seconds | One sentence. A question with a measurable answer. |
| 2. Data | 25 seconds | Source, date range, number of tickers, and one transformation that matters. |
| 3. One chart | 60 seconds | Walk the audience through the axes, then point to the bar or line that answers the question. |
| 4. Finding, with a number | 45 seconds | State the answer as a number with units. Compare it to something. |
| 5. One caveat | 30 seconds | The single biggest reason the finding might not hold. |

Keep the remaining time as buffer. Do not add a second chart.

## Example (from the reference solution)

1. **Question:** Which sector in our universe is the most volatile, and by how much?
2. **Data:** Synthetic daily closes for 8 tickers in 4 sectors, January 2, 2024 to December 5, 2025. Volatility is the 21-day rolling standard deviation of daily log returns, annualized by the square root of 252.
3. **Chart:** Horizontal bar chart of average annualized volatility by sector. The title states the answer: "Technology is the most volatile sector (38.8% annualized)."
4. **Finding:** Technology averages 38.8% annualized volatility, 1.9 times Health Care at 20.4%.
5. **Caveat:** Health Care is a single ticker (HLT1) and the other sectors have two or three, so one name can drive a sector average. TCH2 alone averages 43.1%.

## Checklist before you present

- The chart title states the finding, not the topic.
- Both axes have labels with units.
- The finding contains at least one number with units.
- You can say the caveat in one sentence.
- You rehearsed once with a timer and finished under three minutes.
